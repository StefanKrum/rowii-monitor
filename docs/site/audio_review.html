What do the categories mean?

sustained (fusion path): several seconds contiguously anomalous relative to the normal behavior of the respective operating mode – at least 3 consecutive windows with p < 0.01 (>=3 s). Typical for acoustic anomalies of this kind: a changed timbre, rubbing, flow noise.

transient (BEATs path): a single short impulse, extremely unlikely under the learned normal behavior – a single window with p < 0.001. Typical: a click, a strike, a switching noise.

impulse (band-energy path): a short impulse found directly in the 5–20 kHz band of the raw audio (10 ms resolution, well below the 1 s window the other two paths are limited to), heard on both microphones within 0.15 s of each other – both-mic coincidence is what separates a real plant-wide impulse from a single-sensor artifact. This path exists because per-strike evaluation found the embedding paths above structurally cannot resolve two impulses closer together than about a window width; a candidate already caught by the transient path within 2 s is not listed a second time here.

Each card also states the exact trigger reason (class, window count/duration, minimum p-value) as a sentence below the mode line. Some candidates additionally carry a highlighted context note – a short curated remark (e.g. a cross-reference to the partner team's own, independently reported search window) – shown just below that sentence.

Mode (SCADA) is the operating mode derived rule-based from the operating data (speed, power, flow) for the candidate's time window – the more reliable figure. Mode (Detector) is the mode recognized by the unsupervised state cluster; this can, e.g., mislabel pump-operation windows as “turbine” (a known limitation of the cluster naming), and is generally unreliable near operating transitions. When the two disagree, a warning appears – when in doubt, SCADA governs. The “SCADA: transition/ramp” note additionally flags that the time window itself touches a ramp-up/ramp-down or a power ramp, i.e. the SCADA figure is the majority of a non-uniform set of windows.

A “in-sample (fit-pool day)” badge marks a candidate whose recording day was itself used to fit the detector/scoring model – its evidentiary status is weaker than a genuinely held-out day, shown here rather than left implicit.

SCADA context panel: below the two audio lanes, each card also shows the actual operating data for the same snippet against a shared time axis – active power, shaft speed, net flow, and spherical-valve position – so you can judge what the machine was actually doing, not just what the model flagged. In the interactive tool this is four responsive curves with a playhead that follows the audio, each row showing its own live reading as it plays. Underneath it, a two-row state ribbon shows the SCADA-derived state (top row) and the detector's own state (bottom row) for every second of the snippet, colored per the legend below. 27.06. has no Betriebsdaten at all, so its curves and readings stay blank (the state ribbon's SCADA row is then uniformly grey/“unknown”, correctly, since there is nothing to derive a state from).

Assessment: plausible anomaly = sounds like a genuine anomaly · operational/explained = plausibly explained by normal operation (e.g. a switching event) · artifact/sensor = likely a measurement/sensor artifact · no finding = listened, nothing noteworthy · unclear = not confidently assessable. An honest “no finding” or “unclear” is a full, welcome result – this register is explicitly not ground truth, but a qualitative listening review.

Listen & look: clicking a spectrogram jumps to that position and continues playback from there; the space bar plays/pauses the respective lane (the lane must be focused, e.g. after a click). Two independent lanes per candidate (generator/turbine microphone).

Flag a moment: Shift-click a spectrogram to drop a red time mark at that exact position (in addition to seeking there) – useful for pointing at the specific instant worth a second listen. Marks appear as removable chips under the lanes, persist locally, and are included in the CSV export as a dedicated column.

Assess: choose one assessment per candidate (plausible anomaly / operational-explained / artifact-sensor / unclear) and optionally enter a note – saved locally as you go (shown as “saved”), persists across reloads.

Export: at the end of each session click “Export CSV”, or “Export all sessions” above – this file is the input for candidate_kit.py compile.
